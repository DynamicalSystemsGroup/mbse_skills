# Selecting standards and institutional guidance

First identify the claim type: a binding project obligation, a normative standard rule, informative guidance, methodology convention or implementation observation. Record the authority that makes a source applicable to this project; institutional reputation alone does not create an obligation.

Record title, issuing body, edition/version, artifact ID, official URL, retrieval date, clause/page, and byte hash when retained. Distinguish the logical clause from PDF pagination. Read its scope, dependencies and exceptions. A catalog page identifies a document but usually cannot establish its detailed rule.

Start with the issuing body's official version page. INCOSE guidance, NASA procedures and ISO/IEC/IEEE standards have different scopes and access conditions. Identify the exact edition; paid or inaccessible text remains a source gap. Use original summaries and pointers rather than reconstructing restricted material.

For language semantics choose the language extension and its edition-specific source reference. Engine behavior is evidence about an implementation, not the normative wording. Papers and repositories can inform interpretation but do not replace the applicable standard.
